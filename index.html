your customers get a clicky too. no install.

try it in a live appruns in your browser. nothing to sign up for.

you're a clicky user. your customers aren't. clicky lite is one script tag that drops a clicky-style buddy into any web app: customers ask how to do something, and it flies to the right button and walks them through it.

how it works

live in an afternoon

three steps, no server.

  1. step-1.html

    add one script tag

    paste it into your app. an "ask clicky" button shows up where you put it.

    <script src="clicky-lite.js"
  data-clicky-pack="clicky-pack.json"
  defer></script>
  2. step-2.md

    point it at your help docs

    your help center becomes walkthroughs. numbered steps and bold button names are enough.

    npx clicky-lite pack ./help
4 walkthroughs · 18/18 steps
point at a real button ✓
  3. step-3.mov

    your customers ask

    "how do i send an invoice?" the buddy flies to each button and moves on as they click. "where's harbor ceramics?" it points at the row, or shows the way. anything a guide can't fix goes to your support team, question filled in.

trust.txt

it points. it never clicks. (•‿•)

  • runs in the browser. answers come from a small static file on your own site. questions and screens aren't sent to anyone, not even clicky.
  • only reads what you allow. page titles, visible labels and text outside blocked regions, the controls you tag, errors you mark. never field values, passwords, card numbers, keystrokes or screenshots.
  • never acts for anyone. it can't click, type or submit. your customer does every step.
  • shows its work. one click opens "what clicky lite can see", the exact list, live.

the case for clicky lite

why a company that makes a desktop app should ship a script tag.

the problem

clicky's magic is locked behind an install. it's great when you have it: it sees your screen, talks you through the tool and points at the button. but only people who find it, download it and grant screen access ever get there. that's a small, technical slice of everyone stuck in software right now.

meanwhile, saas teams drown in "how do i…" questions. a big share of tickets for most web apps are things the help center already answers. customers don't read it, don't find it, or can't map "click payment terms" onto the screen in front of them. each one is a ticket, a slow onboarding, or a quiet churn.

the people who need clicky most are the least likely to install it. the companies that serve them would pay to fix that.

who it's for

the buyer: product-led saas teams

web apps with self-serve signup, a help center, and a team that owns activation. invoicing, scheduling, crm, hr, analytics: 5 to 50 core workflows. they already pay for chat widgets and product tours, and they're tired of tours that break on every redesign.

the user: their non-technical customers

the bookkeeper, the studio owner, the clinic manager. they live in a browser, won't install anything, and want the answer on the screen in front of them, not in a help article in another tab.

what we're building

a lightweight, web-only cousin of clicky that lives inside someone else's product:

  • one script tag and a knowledge pack built from the app's existing help docs. no server.
  • an "ask clicky" button the app places where it likes.
  • the buddy flies to the exact control and explains the step in a speech bubble.
  • it moves on when the customer really does the step, and starts from wherever they are.
  • errors on screen get explained ("why did my payment fail?") and the fix gets pointed at.
  • a finder: "where is invoice 1045?", "take me to settings" or just "harbor ceramics" points at the thing, or walks to the page it's on. it never looks inside blocked regions.
  • it gets how people actually ask ("bill a client", "chase late payments", typos too), and offers the closest matches instead of a dead end.
  • anything it can't solve, like refunds or bugs, goes to the app's support email with the question filled in.

the demo runs on the navoro engine, nitai's in-app guidance project, reskinned as clicky lite. it works with no model at all: walkthroughs come straight from the docs, so the buddy never invents a button.

why clicky should build it

distribution
every web app that installs it becomes a surface. millions of people meet clicky, by name, inside tools they already use, without anyone asking them to install anything.
a second revenue line
consumer plans are one line. a per-app plan, priced on monthly active users helped, is a second one that grows with each customer.
brand
"clicky" becomes the word for "show me where to click". every lite session ends with an easy route to the full desktop clicky.
a head start
pointing at the right thing and teaching step by step is exactly what in-app guidance tools do badly. clicky already has the taste for it.

risks

wrong pointing
pointing at the wrong button is worse than no answer. steps only point at controls the app tagged or whose label matches exactly. if nothing matches it says so, and a ci check fails the build when a doc names a button that's gone.
privacy
a guide that "sees your screen" inside someone else's product is a trust problem for two companies at once. local by default, allowlist-first reading, payment and password fields excluded, and the visible "what it can see" panel.
latency
a guide that takes three seconds feels broken. local mode answers in milliseconds. a model, if added, only chooses among known walkthroughs.
brand dilution
a cheaper clicky inside a clunky app could make clicky look clunky. keep the buddy and its voice consistent, cap how far hosts can restyle it, vet early partners.
cannibalisation
lite helps in one app. clicky helps across your whole computer and runs agents. lite is the trailer, not the film.

rollout

  1. dogfood. install it on clicky's own web pages (account, billing, docs). fix pointing until completion is over 60%.
  2. design partners. five to ten saas apps with good help centers, free for a quarter in exchange for holdout data.
  3. self-serve beta. public docs, a pack builder in the browser, a usage dashboard, a free tier.
  4. paid plans and the bridge. per-app pricing, packs that update from the help center, and the "get clicky for your mac" link turned on.

left out on purpose

  • agents that act for the user. lite never clicks, types or submits. acting inside another company's app needs its own consent and security design.
  • voice on day one, unless it's reliable. the demo offers a mic only where the browser supports it.
  • screenshots or pixel vision. it reads labels the app allows, not images of the screen.
  • mobile apps. web first.
  • replacing support. it hands off. it doesn't pretend to be a person.
the flow

screen by screen

for developers

the script, a config, and the help docs you already write. build the pack, ship two static files, done.

<!-- 1. the whole install. serve both files from your own site. -->
<script src="/clicky/clicky-lite.js"
        data-clicky-pack="/clicky/clicky-pack.json"
        data-clicky-accent="#2F6FEA"
        defer></script>

<!-- 2. optional: your own "ask clicky" button. it appears when ready. -->
<button data-clicky-trigger hidden>ask clicky</button>

// 3. optional: open it from code, e.g. from an empty state.
ClickyLite.ask("how do i send an invoice?");
{
  "name": "Fernbill",
  "docs": ["help"],
  "supportEmail": "help@fernbill.example",
  "privacy": {
    "observe": ["route", "pageTitle", "headings", "elements", "visibleErrors"],
    "neverCollect": [
      { "clickyId": "settings.payout", "reason": "Bank account details" },
      { "selector": "[data-fernbill-private]", "reason": "Client notes" }
    ]
  }
}

$ npm run pack

  never read: settings.payout, [data-fernbill-private]
## How to …  with numbered steps becomes a walkthrough.
## bold text names the button to point at.
## How to send an invoice

1. Open **Invoices** from the sidebar.
2. Click **New invoice** at the top of the Invoices page.
3. Choose a **Client** from your client list.
4. Pick **Payment terms**, such as Net 30.
5. Click **Send invoice**.

Also asked as: bill a client, create an invoice, make an invoice

## Troubleshooting: message (CODE)  explains an error and points at the fix.
## Troubleshooting: Payment failed: card declined (PAYMENT_DECLINED)

Resolution: Click **Resend payment link** so your client can try another card.
<!-- optional but recommended: stable ids survive redesigns. -->
<a href="/invoices" data-clicky-id="nav.invoices">Invoices</a>
<button data-clicky-id="invoices.new">New invoice</button>

<!-- mark errors so "what's wrong?" can explain them. -->
<p data-clicky-error="PAYMENT_DECLINED" data-clicky-error-for="payments.resend">
  Payment failed: card declined
</p>

<!-- never read, whatever the config says. -->
<section data-clicky-sensitive>…</section>
