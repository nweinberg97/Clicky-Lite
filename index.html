trust.txt

it points. it never clicks. (•‿•)

  • only sees this one app. nothing else on your computer, no other tabs, no github. it knows the app from the docs its maker gives it.
  • runs in the browser. answers come from a small file on the app's own site. nothing to download, and questions and screens aren't sent anywhere.
  • only reads what you allow. page titles, visible labels and text outside blocked regions, the controls you tag, errors you mark. never field values, passwords, card numbers, keystrokes or screenshots.
  • never acts for anyone. it can't click, type or submit. your user does every step.

the case for clicky lite

a product sense exercise: why a desktop ai buddy could also ship as a help button for other people's web apps.

where this came from

i'm a paying clicky user. i'm non-technical by background, and i mostly use clicky to get unstuck: when i don't know how to do something, like backend work or building agents, it shows me on screen. it's helped me build 40 prototypes.

then i ran into something. i wanted clicky to really understand one of my own web apps, but i didn't want to connect all my github, because some of it is private. in claude i can link one specific repo; in clicky i can't.

so i wondered: what if clicky could live inside the web app, already knowing that app and only seeing that app? once i learned how sdks and embeds work, the bigger idea clicked: anyone using that app could get clicky's help without downloading anything.

i'd already built something like this for my own apps, called navoro (my own separate project). this page is a mockup of what it could look like as clicky, made to share the idea. it isn't something i'm launching. the demo is sedi, one of my apps, which so far has a handful of users, mostly friends and family.

the problem

clicky's magic is locked behind an install. it's great when you have it: it sees your screen, talks you through the tool and points at the button. but only people who find it, download it and grant screen access ever get there. that's a small, technical slice of everyone stuck in software right now.

meanwhile, every web app gets "how do i…" questions. most of them are already answered in the help docs. users don't read them, don't find them, or can't map "click scheduling" onto the screen in front of them. each one is a support message, a slow start, or someone quietly leaving.

the people who need clicky most are the least likely to install it. the people building those apps would love to fix that.

who it's for

the buyer: product-led saas teams

web apps with self-serve signup, a help center, and a team that owns activation. invoicing, scheduling, crm, hr, analytics: 5 to 50 core workflows. they already pay for chat widgets and product tours, and they're tired of tours that break on every redesign.

the user: the people using those apps

the bookkeeper, the studio owner, the clinic manager. they live in a browser, won't install anything, and want the answer on the screen in front of them, not in a help article in another tab.

the idea

a lightweight, web-only clicky that lives behind a help button in someone else's app:

  • private by design: it only sees that one app, nothing else.
  • knows the app already: it learns from the app's docs and readme, so users don't have to explain their situation.
  • shows, doesn't just tell: ask how to do something and it walks you through it on screen, in clicky's style.
  • one script tag and a knowledge pack built from the app's existing help docs. no server.
  • a help button the app puts in its own header, like the one in sedi's top right.
  • the buddy flies to the exact control and explains the step in a speech bubble.
  • it moves on when the user really does the step, and starts from wherever they are.
  • errors on screen get explained and the fix gets pointed at.
  • a finder: "where is timely?" or "open brainly" points at the thing on screen. it never looks inside blocked regions, like your notes and cards.
  • it gets how people actually ask, typos too, and offers the closest matches instead of a dead end.
  • anything it can't solve, like bugs, goes to the app's support with the question filled in.

the demo is real: it's sedi, my workspace app, where navoro is already the help button. here it's reskinned as clicky lite and reads sedi's actual help center (75 walkthroughs). most questions are matched right in the browser; when it isn't sure, claude picks one of sedi's own walkthroughs. it never invents a step or a button.

why it could make sense for clicky

a growth loop
people try clicky inside apps they already use, without downloading anything. if they love it, they get the full clicky. every app that adds it becomes a way in.
better than what's out there
in-app tours (pendo, walkme, intercom) are scripted by hand and break when the app changes. support chatbots repeat the help docs back at you. clicky's on-screen guidance could do this much better.
brand
"clicky" becomes the word for "show me where to click".

risks

wrong pointing
pointing at the wrong button is worse than no answer. steps only point at controls the app tagged or whose label matches exactly. if nothing matches it says so, and a ci check fails the build when a doc names a button that's gone.
privacy
a guide that "sees your screen" inside someone else's product is a trust problem for two companies at once. local by default, allowlist-first reading, payment and password fields excluded, and the visible "what it can see" panel.
latency
a guide that takes three seconds feels broken. local mode answers in milliseconds. a model, if added, only chooses among known walkthroughs.
brand dilution
a cheaper clicky inside a clunky app could make clicky look clunky. keep the buddy and its voice consistent, cap how far hosts can restyle it, vet early partners.
cannibalisation
lite helps in one app. clicky helps across your whole computer and runs agents. lite is the trailer, not the film.

how i'd roll it out

  1. dogfood. install it on clicky's own web pages (account, billing, docs). fix pointing until completion is over 60%.
  2. design partners. five to ten web apps with good help docs, free for a quarter in exchange for holdout data.
  3. self-serve beta. public docs, a pack builder in the browser and a usage dashboard.
  4. the bridge. packs that update from the help center by themselves, and the "get clicky for your mac" link turned on.

what i left out on purpose

  • agents that act for the user. lite never clicks, types or submits. acting inside another company's app needs its own consent and security design.
  • voice on day one, unless it's reliable. the demo offers a mic only where the browser supports it.
  • screenshots or pixel vision. it reads labels the app allows, not images of the screen.
  • mobile apps. web first.
  • replacing support. it hands off. it doesn't pretend to be a person.
the flow

screen by screen

for developers

the script, a config, and the help docs you already write. build the pack, ship two static files, done.

<!-- 1. the whole install. serve both files from your own site. -->
<script src="/clicky/clicky-lite.js"
        data-clicky-pack="/clicky/clicky-pack.json"
        defer></script>

<!-- 2. your help button. ship it hidden; it appears when clicky lite is ready. -->
<button data-clicky-trigger hidden>help</button>

// 3. optional: open it from code, e.g. from an empty state.
ClickyLite.ask("how do i add a reminder?");
// sedi's real config (trimmed). blocked regions are never read.
{
  "name": "Sedi",
  "docs": ["help/help-center.md"],
  "privacy": {
    "observe": ["route", "pageTitle", "elements", "visibleErrors"],
    "neverCollect": [
      { "selector": ".col-body, .task-card", "reason": "Taskly cards" },
      { "selector": ".brain-canvas", "reason": "Brainly notes and links" },
      { "selector": ".modal, .popover", "reason": "Editors hold what you type" }
    ]
  }
}

## How to add a reminder

1. Choose **Timely** in the tool buttons (right edge, or the bottom bar on phones).
2. Click **Reminders**.
3. Type into **New reminder** on the blank note.
4. Pick a time: **In 1 hour**, **Tonight**, **Tomorrow 9am**, **Saturday**, or **Date & time**.
5. Click **Pin** or press Enter.
<!-- optional but recommended: stable ids survive redesigns. -->
<button data-clicky-id="nav.timely">Timely</button>

<!-- mark errors so "what's wrong?" can explain them. -->
<div class="toast" data-clicky-error="no-idb">…</div>

<!-- never read, whatever the config says. sedi marks every editor this way. -->
<div class="modal" data-clicky-sensitive>…</div>
