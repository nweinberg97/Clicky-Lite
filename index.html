why-i-made-this.txt

why i made this

i'm a non-technical builder, and i use clicky to get unstuck. it's helped me build 40 prototypes.

i wanted clicky to know one of my web apps, without connecting all my github. so i wondered: what if clicky lived inside the app, and only saw that app? then anyone using the app could get clicky's help, with nothing to download.

this is a mockup of that idea, built on navoro, my own project. it's not something i'm launching. the demo is sedi, one of my apps.

the idea

a clicky inside the app

  • private.txt

    only sees one app

    nothing else on your computer. it reads button names, never what you've written.

  • knows.md

    already knows it

    it learns from the app's own docs, so nobody has to explain their situation.

  • shows.mov

    shows, doesn't tell

    ask "how do i…?" and a little buddy points at each button as you go.

why it could matter for clicky: people try clicky inside apps they already use, with no download, and the ones who love it get the full clicky. and it beats hand-scripted tours (pendo, walkme, intercom) and chatbots that repeat the docs.

how it works

  1. 1

    add one script tag

    the app maker pastes it in and marks their help button.

    <script src="clicky-lite.js" data-clicky-pack="clicky-pack.json" defer></script>
  2. 2

    point it at the docs

    the help docs they already have become step-by-step walkthroughs.

  3. 3

    users press help

    they ask in their own words. it points, and moves on as they click. it never clicks for them.

product-notes.txt · problem, metrics, risks, rollout

the problem

clicky's help is locked behind an install, and the people who need it most won't install anything. meanwhile every web app gets "how do i…?" questions its docs already answer.

how i'd know it works

more new users reach the app's first "aha" moment · fewer "how do i" support messages · most walkthroughs finished · people who go on to get the full clicky.

risks

pointing at the wrong button (so it only points at exact matches, and says so when it can't) · trust inside someone else's app (so it reads only what the app allows) · a clunky host app making clicky look clunky.

rollout

try it on clicky's own web pages first, then a handful of apps with good docs, then let any builder add it.
